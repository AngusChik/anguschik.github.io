Problem

AIS affects children aged 10–18 and causes cascading biomechanical dysfunction. The challenge was mapping how a single spinal deformity propagates through posture, gait, lung function, and spatial awareness as one interconnected system.

Process

  • Reviewed literature on thoracic curve mechanics, muscle volume asymmetry, and spinal compression forces
  • Traced the kinetic chain from spinal malalignment through pelvic tilt, leg length discrepancy, and gait disruption
  • Compared treatment outcomes across Schroth exercise, at-home management, and nighttime bracing studies

Learnings

The body compensates at every level. AIS doesn't just affect the spine — it reshapes muscle tone, lung capacity, spatial awareness, and gait mechanics. Understanding the full kinetic chain made clear why early, compliant intervention matters so much before skeletal maturity.

01 Research 2024

A biomechanical review of Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis — tracing how spinal deformity disrupts posture, stability, gait, and respiratory function across the kinetic chain.

AIS produces muscle volume asymmetry on the concave and convex sides of the curve, uneven spinal compression leading to disc herniation risk, pelvic malalignment, and leg length discrepancy. These compound into gait changes — reduced step length, altered force transmission, and increased mechanical work — alongside a measurable 5% lung function difference between sides. Treatment analysis compared the Schroth method, non-surgical home management, and bracing protocols across Cobb angle progression data.

Outcome

A clear biomechanical framework for how AIS progresses and why early corrective intervention is essential.
