Problem

How do you create a brand language that stays legible across digital and print without relying on colour as the primary differentiator?

Process

  • Established a wordmark grid using optical spacing rather than fixed margins
  • Chose a warm-neutral palette to allow imagery to lead without competition
  • Stress-tested the system at small scales and in reversed contexts before finalising

Learnings

Restraint is harder than decoration. Removing elements until only the necessary remained forced sharper decisions about what the brand actually needed to communicate.

01 Branding 2026

A stripped-back brand language for a modern studio built around quiet typography, careful spacing, and a warm-neutral palette.

The system needed to move fluidly between digital surfaces and print collateral without losing coherence. It centres on a single wordmark, a constrained type palette, and spacing rules derived from the mark itself — ensuring every application feels considered rather than adapted.

Deliverables

  • Wordmark & lockup variations
  • Colour & typography system
  • Digital component library
  • Brand usage guidelines

Outcome

A brand system that feels calm, direct, and editorial.
